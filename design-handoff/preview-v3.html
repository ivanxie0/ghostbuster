Your insurance directory is full of ghosts.

We find out who's actually taking patients. If nobody can see you in time, we build the evidence to make your plan fix it.

It's free, and you don't need an account. We never ask for your name or health details, and nobody gets called until you say so.

From our live test run: of 90 licensed therapists listed near 92103 (Hillcrest, San Diego), 5 listings appear to be ghosts.

Built on public sources: California Health & Safety Code §1367.03, the national NPI registry, and the DMHC grievance process. GhostBuster is an independent student project and isn't affiliated with any agency or health plan.

Finding a therapist who takes your insurance shouldn't be a second job.

01

02

The phone says no.

Then you start calling. One office stopped taking new patients. One moved away. One number is disconnected. One never calls back. These are ghost listings, and you find out one call at a time.

03

The law is on your side.

California's timely-access rules set deadlines for state-regulated plans: 10 business days for a therapist or primary care appointment, 15 for a specialist, 48 hours for urgent care that needs no prior authorization. When a plan can't offer an appointment in time, it has to arrange one for you, including out of network.

04

But the proof is on you.

To use those rules, you need to show the plan failed: who you called, when, and what they said. GhostBuster does that legwork for you and writes it down.

We make the calls. You keep the receipts.

Common questions

Will you call anyone without asking me?

No. Calls only start after you approve them on the results page. You'll see exactly which offices will be called first.

What does the AI say on the phone?

It says it's an AI assistant calling on behalf of a patient and that the call is being transcribed, then asks only for the earliest opening, in person or by telehealth. It doesn't share your name, contact info, or health details.

Is this legal advice?

No. You get an evidence log and letter templates, not legal advice. What you do with them is your call.

Does this work for my plan?

California's deadlines apply to state-regulated coverage. They don't apply to self-funded employer or union plans, or to Medicare. Not sure? Ask HR or member services "Is my plan self-funded?"

What do you store about me?

Your search (insurer, plan type, ZIP, type of care) and the call results. No account, no name, no health details.
